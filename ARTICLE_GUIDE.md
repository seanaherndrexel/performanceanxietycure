# Article rules for performanceanxietycure.com

Every article exists to rank on Google for one keyword and send readers to buy The Performance Anxiety Cure by Sean Stephens on Amazon.

## File format
Create `posts/YYYY-MM-DD-<slug>.html` (today's date, America/New_York). Body is bare HTML: `<p>`, `<h2>`, `<h3>`, `<ul>/<li>`, `<blockquote>`, `<strong>`. No `<h1>`, no html/head/body tags, no inline styles, no images. Start with this front matter comment:

```
<!--
title: <SEO title, 50-65 chars, keyword near the front>
description: <meta description, 140-158 chars, includes keyword>
date: YYYY-MM-DD
slug: <keyword-as-slug>
keywords: <main keyword>, <3-5 close variations>
-->
```

The site builder adds the headline, buy boxes after paragraph three, the sidebar, related posts and the closing call to action automatically. Every page already pushes the book, so the article itself mentions the book naturally two or three times, never as a hard sell in every paragraph.

## Length and structure
- 1,200 to 1,800 words.
- Open cold on a concrete, blunt claim. No throat-clearing intro.
- Use the keyword in the first 100 words, in one H2, and naturally 3 to 5 more times. No stuffing.
- 5 to 8 H2 sections. Headings must not be on the nose (don't literally restate the section's point).
- End with a short practical summary (a list is fine), then one or two sentences pointing to the book.
- Link internally with relative links to 2 or 3 method pages where they fit: /method/stop-feeding-the-fear/, /method/the-fear-switch/, /method/oxygen-mask-first/, /method/say-it-out-loud/, /method/fail-on-purpose/, /method/the-first-thirty-seconds/, /method/skip-the-shortcuts/, /method/training-wheels/. Link to one earlier blog post when relevant.

## Voice (hard rules)
- Forbes contributor / industry operator voice. Declarative, active, varied sentence length.
- Absolutely no em dashes or en dashes anywhere. Use commas, periods or parentheses.
- Banned words and phrases: without the fluff, hype, unlock, leverage, seamless, effortless, game-changer, supercharge, delve, dive into, deep dive, move the needle, crucial, pivotal, robust, empower, curated, journey, ultimately, when it comes to, that said, in conclusion, why this matters, why X matters, actually (in headings).
- No "X isn't just Y, it's Z" reversals. No sentence fragments for emphasis. No rhetorical questions as transitions. Write in full sentences.
- No invented studies, statistics, percentages, doctors or quotes. Attribute general claims journalist-style ("urologists commonly describe...", "sex therapists call this..."). If you state a hard fact, it must be well established.
- Never steer readers to alternatives to the book (no competing books, apps, programs or brand-name supplements).
- Medical safety: any mention of medication says to talk to a doctor first. Never give dosing. Encourage safer sex; never suggest skipping condoms. Tasteful, not explicit.
- No author byline or bio in the article. Don't invent facts about Sean Stephens.
- Never mention Spider-Man or other trademarks, Main Street Creative, or any other business.
