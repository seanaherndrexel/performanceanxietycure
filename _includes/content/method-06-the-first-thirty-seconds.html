Men with performance anxiety report a pattern so consistent it's almost a rule: once they're actually inside, even for a moment, the anxiety drops away. The proof is there. She knows it, he knows it, and the pressure collapses.

So the hard part isn't sex itself. It's the short stretch of time leading up to it.

Where erections get lost

That window, from getting hard to the start of intercourse, is where the fear hits hardest. Your mind is watching every sensation, waiting for a sign that things are fading. That level of self-monitoring is exactly what makes things fade.

The common places it goes wrong are the condom, repositioning, and the pause between foreplay and sex. None of these mean you're not attracted to her. They're simply the highest-pressure seconds of the night, and they give your brain time to interfere.

Shrink the thinking time

The principle is simple: make the transition as short and low-stress as possible, and remove every step that hands your mind an opening. Fewer pauses, fewer setups, less time to stand there and watch yourself.

The book lays out two specific techniques that do this, including a position that lets your partner guide things so you can stay in the sensation instead of managing logistics. Both cut down the gap your anxiety lives in and increase physical connection, which calms the nervous system on its own.

You don't need to win the whole night. You need to get through one short window, and you can make that window much smaller.

Condoms deserve their own plan

Even men without anxiety find condoms interrupt momentum. For an anxious man, stopping to open a wrapper and look down at the worst possible moment is a classic trigger. The book has a full chapter on handling this safely and honestly, including how to talk to a partner about testing and protection. Safer sex isn't optional, and the book never suggests treating it that way. It shows you how to plan around the trigger instead of being ambushed by it.

Once a man gets through the window successfully a few times, his brain updates its story. The fear has a harder time arguing with evidence.
