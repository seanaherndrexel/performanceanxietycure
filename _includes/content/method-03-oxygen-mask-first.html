A lot of men walk into sex with a job description. Make her happy. Prove you can get hard and stay hard. Show you know what you're doing. It sounds generous, and it's one of the main reasons the anxiety takes hold.

You can't get aroused by trying to perform. You can only get aroused by feeling good. Erections aren't produced by effort. They're produced by enjoyment.

The paradox

Here's the trap. You focus on her because you want her to have a good time. That focus pulls attention away from your own pleasure. Without pleasure, your body shuts down. When your body shuts down, she doesn't have a good time either. The thing you did to help her is what hurt both of you.

The book uses the airline rule: put your own oxygen mask on before helping anyone else. Taking care of your own arousal first isn't selfish. It's the only way the experience can happen at all.

She feels the difference

Women tend to notice when a man is truly enjoying himself. His excitement and his presence are a big part of what makes her feel wanted. A man who is performing, running through steps he thinks she expects, comes across as distant and tense, and that tension spreads.

When you're engaged in what feels good to you, you're more present, more connected and more attractive. Your pleasure and hers aren't competing. Yours is the foundation hers gets built on.

What to focus on instead

Shift your attention from "am I doing this right?" to "what feels good right now?" A few things help:

  • Touch for your own enjoyment. Touch is grounding. It pulls you out of your head and into your body, and you don't need to be hard to do it.
  • Use your voice. Saying what you like and what you want keeps you in the moment instead of drifting into worry.
  • Take the clock away. Foreplay isn't a race to an erection. Being soft for a while during it doesn't matter.
Erections come from pleasure. Pleasure comes from being present. Being present comes from taking your attention off the fear.

Confidence in this context doesn't mean pretending everything is perfect. It means letting yourself be real, nervous or not, and moving toward what feels good anyway.
