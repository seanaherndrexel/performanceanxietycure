For some men the fear shows up long before anything happens. It starts on the drive over. By the time they're alone with someone, their mind has already decided how the night will end. No amount of attraction or good chemistry can get through that.

The Fail On Purpose reset is built for exactly this state.

Walk in expecting nothing

Instead of going in determined to perform, you go in accepting that you might not get hard, and you're fine with that. You're not there to prove anything. You're there to enjoy touching, kissing and being close to someone.

You make this real by saying it, casually, before things heat up:

"I get nervous sometimes, so if I'm not instantly hard just ignore it. I still want to have fun."

That sentence does two jobs. It protects her from assuming she's the problem, and it tells your own brain that there's no test tonight.

Pressure can't hook into you

When you expect to fail, there's nothing for the anxiety to grab. You're not chasing an erection or waiting for your body to start working. You stop checking yourself like a gauge on a dashboard. Your attention goes back to the person in front of you, and that's the environment where arousal grows on its own.

The irony surprises most men. The moment they stop caring whether an erection shows up, it often does. Their body responds to honesty and comfort far better than it ever responded to pressure.

Use it early

This reset is most powerful the first time you're with someone. Without it, a first encounter can become the perfect storm: you're anxious, she thinks it's her fault, the mood turns, and that memory hangs over every future attempt with her. Starting with honesty keeps the night playful instead of tense.

Giving yourself permission to fail is how you take your power back. You remove the expectation to perform on demand, and once that weight is gone, your body is free to do what it already knows how to do.
